Tests of out-of-sample predictive ability compare a model against a benchmark, and handle the outcome's unconditional drift by plugging in an estimate of it. When the benchmark is the zero forecast, the resulting statistic loads on that drift: we show by simulation that Clark-West rejects a pure-noise signal 81% of the time at the nominal 5% level once the asset drifts at an annualised Sharpe ratio of 2. We give an e-process that removes the drift instead of estimating it, by taking an infimum over the nuisance of a convex combination of a signal-betting martingale and a drift-centring martingale. Ville's inequality then bounds the rejection probability at alpha in finite samples, uniformly in time and uniformly in the unknown drift; measured, it never exceeds 1% across the same sweep. We report what the construction costs rather than only what it fixes: against a correctly specified Clark-West test it needs roughly twice the sample at matched size, and anytime-validity itself accounts for a factor of 1.90. Inverting the process over its value parameter yields a time-uniform upper bound on incremental predictive value, which is available whether or not anything is rejected. Applied to a century of equity-premium data it certifies nothing, and the median of its marginal 95% upper endpoints on incremental annualised information ratio is 0.60.
